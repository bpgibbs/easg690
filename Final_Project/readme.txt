Natural Science:

In my program, I am tracking isotope ratio values of Thallium (Tl) and their normalized "epsilon" values
through different parts of brown mustard plants. The epsilon value is just a normalized ratio of Tl isotope ratios
for better readability. The heavy metal isotopes lab at IU is currently studying these isotope ratios because
increased 205/203 Thallium isotope ratios (aka higher sigma values) are associated with a much more toxic form
of the metal. Additionally, by learning what the isotope "signature" looks like through brown mustard (the
pattern of how the ratios change through the plant), we believe we can predict minerals under the plants and
also trace thallium contamination sources. Brown mustard is ideal for this kind of work because it hyperaccumulates
thallium... that is, it sucks A LOT of thallium into itself from the soils.
